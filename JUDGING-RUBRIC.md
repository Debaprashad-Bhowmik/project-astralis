# 2026 NASA Space Apps judging rubric

Judges rate each of the five criteria from **1 to 5** at every round, from Local and Universal Event judging through Executive Judging. The descriptions below paraphrase NASA's published criteria and top-score examples. [Official 2026 Judging & Awards Guide](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/resources/judging-awards-guide/#judging-criteria)

| Criterion | What judges look for | What a 5 demonstrates |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Impact** | How significant the problem is and how much the solution could help or inspire its audience. | The work addresses a significant problem and offers exceptional benefits, reach, or potential for change. |
| **Creativity** | How original the idea or execution is, including meaningful improvements to existing approaches. | An inventive approach breaks new ground and distinguishes the solution from existing work. |
| **Validity** | Whether the solution is scientifically sound, feasible, functional, and usable. | A complete, practical solution applies sound science, works as intended, and is easy to use. |
| **Relevance** | How directly the solution addresses its chosen challenge and how meaningfully it uses NASA data. | The solution addresses the challenge in depth, with NASA data driving its core functionality or findings. |
| **Presentation** | How effectively the demo and full project explain the problem, solution, and significance. | Clear, polished storytelling makes the solution and its impact easy to understand. |

## Evidence Astralis can show

- **Impact** — Show a site or schedule change and explain what a different sunlight or Earth visibility window means for a mission planner.
- **Creativity** — Show how a shared timeline makes the trade-off between two scenarios easy to see.
- **Validity** — Show a reproducible check against JPL Horizons. State the horizon model, sampling interval, and assumptions used in the prototype.
- **Relevance** — Show side-by-side landing site or date comparisons driven by NASA data, with Sun and Earth positions plotted relative to the horizon.
- **Presentation** — Demo one complete decision and make the method, NASA data links, and result easy to find on the project page.

## Submission requirements

Provide a public demo link and a public final project link. The demo can be a video up to **30 seconds** or a deck up to **7 slides**, including the title and introduction. For Global Award eligibility, use at least one NASA data source or resource and explain how the project uses it. The project submission is the main evaluation material, and the demo also informs the Presentation score.
