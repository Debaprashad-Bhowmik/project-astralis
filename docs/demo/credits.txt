LUNAR LANDING SITE NAVIGATOR — TEAM ASTRALIS — 30-SECOND DEMO, REVISION 2

Purpose
An annotated, narrated demonstration of the user's existing Lunar Landing Site Navigator application. It presents the orbital-to-surface journey, walking, mission-specific candidate patch selection, and three recommendation scenarios. It is a condensed edited demonstration, not a recording of a single uninterrupted user session.

Identity and novelty
Astralis is the team; Lunar Landing Site Navigator is the project name (README.md and PRODUCT.md). The narration begins “We are Team Astralis!” and calls the team’s contribution a “NOVEL patch finder, beyond NASA’s preset landing sites.” Novel refers to the mission-specific candidate search implemented by this team, not a claim that the coordinates have never been studied.

Scientific wording verified
1. Solar elevation and sunlight duration are different quantities. For the application's horizontal panel model, unblocked direct-beam power scales with max(0, sin(solar elevation)), with a Sun-distance correction. Terrain shadow and total eclipse produce zero direct power. The code integrates this power across the mission; average power is integrated energy divided by the full mission duration. It does not rank by the arithmetic mean of elevation angles.
   Code: planner.mjs, flatPanelFactor, siteSolarPower, solarExposure; landing-suggestions.mjs, landingPower.
   Primary reference for beam projection: https://pvpmc.sandia.gov/modeling-guide/1-weather-design-inputs/plane-of-array-poa-irradiance/calculating-poa-irradiance/poa-beam/
2. The NASA Space Apps Artemis reference presentation specifies surface slope for landing over 100 m of less than 10 DEGREES. This is not 10 percent grade. The film uses degrees.
   Primary source: NASA Artemis Human Landing System Overview, page 21 (zero-based PDF page 20).
   https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20250009323/downloads/ArtemisOverview2025IntSPAPPSChallenge.pdf
3. Earth-view percentage is displayed. The landing patch ranking specifically uses the longest continuous Earth-view interval. The communications scenario requires an uninterrupted interval covering the whole mission. The balance score combines normalized mission energy and longest continuous Earth-view share geometrically.
   Code: landing-suggestions.mjs, rankLandingSuggestions.
4. The candidate inventory includes 6,685 sampled patches in 84°S–90°S. These are candidate locations beyond the fixed named-site list. The demo does not assert that these coordinates have never been studied by NASA or that the app certifies safe landings. Slope is one screening factor; full mission design and hazard assessment require more analysis.
   Terrain data and slope baseline: https://pgda.gsfc.nasa.gov/products/90
5. The official challenge specifically centers on the lunar South Pole and its extreme lighting and communication constraints. It asks teams to compare dates/sites using Sun/Earth horizon geometry for power and direct Earth communication.
   https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/challenges/lunar-landing-site-navigator/
   The indexed official challenge brief was available during research; direct page fetch returned 502.
